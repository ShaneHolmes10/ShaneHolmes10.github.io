## What even is this research about?

This paper is concerned with a concept in robotics called synchronization. From the highest possible level, a mobile robot is a robot that can move through it's environment (riveting). Robots can move through their environments in all sorts of different ways, they can fly, swim, drive, walk, etc. One super important thing that we as roboticists want to do is to tell a robot to go from one place to another and have it sort of *figure it out*. 

The way a robot *figures this out* is roughly similar to how you would figure out how to get from one end of town to the other end. You would need (A) The directions to get there and (B) Actually know where you are. As you move through the towns it's imperative that you actually know what street your on or your coordinates on earth *as* you're moving around. This process of determining where you are in space is called **localization**. There's all sorts of different ways to localize yourself one way is GPS which tells you your exact coordinates on earth, another way to localize yourself is something called Dead Reckoning.

The idea behind Dead Reckoning is that you start from some known position and as you travel you keep track of your movements. Say you start at (0, 0) and each of your strides is a meter in length, as you move you note how many strides you took. 5 Strides forward, turn 90 degrees to the left then 2 stride. by accumulating your strides you can deduce that you're now at position (5, 2). Robots can also do something like this using something called an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). An IMU can measure very subtle changes in velocity and record those changes, it can do this for orientation as well. So by using an IMU init as a robot moves through it's environment it can make a measurement of its acceleration and the time the measurement was recorded. From that we can really simply calculate the distance and direction traveled during the robots movement. With enough of these measurements and the starting point we can actually recalculate the robots trajectory. This process of taking all of these relative measurements and using them to reconstruct the robots trajectory through space is called **synchronization** This technique is great because it's completely self contained, we don't need access to GPS satellites.
